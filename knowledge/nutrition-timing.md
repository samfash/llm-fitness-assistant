id: nutrition-timing
title: Pre- and post-workout nutrition and caffeine
source: Paraphrased from the ISSN position stands on nutrient timing (2017) and caffeine (2021)
---
Total daily food intake matters far more than precise timing. Before long or intense sessions, a meal or snack containing carbohydrate, eaten one to three hours earlier, can help performance. Heavy or high-fat meals right before training may cause discomfort for some people.

After training, a meal with protein and carbohydrate within a few hours supports recovery. The so-called anabolic window is wide, so there is no need to rush.

Moderate caffeine intake, for example from coffee or a supplement, can improve endurance and strength performance for many people. Studies typically use doses of about 3 to 6 milligrams per kilogram of body weight taken around 60 minutes before exercise. Sensitivity varies, and caffeine late in the day can harm sleep.
