---
id: who-aerobic
title: Aerobic activity guidelines for adults
source: Paraphrased from the WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour (2020)
---
Adults aged 18 to 64 are advised to do at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, or at least 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity, or an equivalent mix of the two. Going beyond 300 minutes of moderate activity per week brings additional health benefits.

Moderate intensity means you can talk but not sing, for example brisk walking or easy cycling. Vigorous intensity means you can only say a few words at a time, for example running or fast cycling. One minute of vigorous activity counts roughly the same as two minutes of moderate activity.

Adults should also do muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity that work all major muscle groups on two or more days per week. Any physical activity is better than none, and sitting time should be limited and replaced with movement of any intensity. People who are inactive can start with small amounts and build up gradually.
