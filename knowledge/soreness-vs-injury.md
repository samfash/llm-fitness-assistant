id: soreness-vs-injury
title: Muscle soreness versus warning signs of injury
source: General sports-medicine consensus summary; not a substitute for medical advice
---
Delayed onset muscle soreness, DOMS, is a dull, widespread ache in the worked muscles that usually starts several hours after unfamiliar exercise and peaks around 24 to 72 hours later. It is common after new exercises or a big increase in volume, such as squats, and usually eases with gentle movement and fades within a few days.

Warning signs that deserve attention include sharp or highly localized pain during a movement, swelling of a joint, numbness or tingling, a joint that gives way, and pain that is getting worse after a week or is present at rest or at night. These are good reasons to stop the aggravating exercise and see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath or pain with sudden swelling in one leg need urgent medical care.
