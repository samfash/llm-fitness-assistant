title: Energy balance, weight loss and weight fluctuations
source: General nutrition consensus summary; verify against primary sources before relying on it
---
Body weight changes over time when you consistently eat more or less energy than you use. A moderate calorie deficit, often around 10 to 25 percent below maintenance, is more sustainable than an aggressive one. A commonly suggested rate of fat loss is roughly 0.25 to 1 percent of body weight per week; faster loss increases the risk of muscle loss, fatigue and poor adherence.

Do not eat below a safe minimum without medical supervision. Very low daily intakes can cause nutrient deficiencies and are not appropriate for most people.

Daily scale weight fluctuates, often by one to two kilograms, because of water, salt, carbohydrate intake, digestive contents and hormones. A sudden jump overnight is usually not fat gain. Judge progress by weekly averages of body weight, waist measurements and training performance rather than single readings.
