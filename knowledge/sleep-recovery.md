title: Sleep, rest days and recovery
source: Paraphrased from the AASM and Sleep Research Society consensus statement (2015) plus general training practice
---
Adults are advised to sleep at least 7 hours per night on a regular basis, and many people feel best with 7 to 9 hours. Short or poor sleep can reduce performance, slow recovery and increase hunger and cravings.

Muscles adapt during recovery, not during the workout itself. Many people can train the same muscle group again after about 48 to 72 hours, depending on how sore they are and how hard they trained. If a muscle is still very sore or performance is dropping, give it more time or lower the volume.

Include one to two rest days per week. Work, life stress and illness all add to total load, so a hard week outside the gym is a good reason to train a little lighter.
