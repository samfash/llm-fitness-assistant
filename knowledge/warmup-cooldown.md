title: Warm-up and cool-down
source: General sports-science consensus summary; verify against primary sources before relying on it
---
A good warm-up takes about 5 to 10 minutes. Begin with light general activity such as brisk walking, cycling or rowing to raise body temperature and heart rate, then do movement drills that mimic the session, such as bodyweight squats or band pull-aparts.

Before the first heavy lift, do a few ramp-up sets with progressively heavier weights, for example around 50, 70 and 85 percent of your working weight for a few reps each. This prepares the movement pattern without causing fatigue.

Long static stretching immediately before lifting or sprinting may temporarily reduce force output in some people, so it is better saved for after training or a separate session. A cool-down such as easy walking is optional; it is comfortable for many people but is not essential for recovery.
