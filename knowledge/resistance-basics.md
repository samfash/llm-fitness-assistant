title: Resistance training basics
source: General sports-science consensus summary; verify against primary sources before relying on it
---
Resistance training builds strength and muscle when muscles are worked hard enough and repeatedly over time. Training each muscle group about twice per week is commonly recommended, because spreading volume across the week tends to work at least as well as one long session.

For muscle growth, many programs use roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group per week, though beginners often make good progress on much less and individual needs vary. A hard set is one taken close enough to failure that the last few reps are challenging.

Build programs around compound movement patterns: a squat or lunge, a hip hinge such as a deadlift, a horizontal push such as a bench press or push-up, a horizontal pull such as a row, and a vertical push or pull such as an overhead press or pull-up. Rest one to three minutes between heavy compound sets so that performance stays high. Learn technique with light loads first.
